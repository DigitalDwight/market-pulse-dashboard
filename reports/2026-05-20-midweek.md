## MACRO THEME

Moody's stripped the United States of its final Aaa sovereign rating (downgraded to Aa1, announced Friday 16 May) -- the last of the three major agencies to remove the top grade following S&P in 2011 and Fitch in 2023. The initial market reaction was a two-session risk-off episode: 10-year Treasury yields spiked toward the 4.58% area, equities sold off Monday-Tuesday, and gold dipped toward 4,465 intraday. The shock was absorbed faster than the scale of the event warranted fearing. By Wednesday May 20 an oil price pullback compressed energy-driven inflation concerns and the Dow closed back above 50,000 for the first time since May 7. The week's dominant catalyst then arrived after Wednesday's close: NVIDIA reported Q1 FY2027 results one session earlier than the prior report's Thursday May 22 expectation. Revenue came in at a record $81.6 billion (+85% year-over-year), Data Center revenue at $75.2 billion (+92% YoY), with an additional $80 billion share repurchase authorization and a dividend raised twenty-five-fold from $0.01 to $0.25 per quarter. The comprehensive blowout confirmed the AI capex supercycle thesis and drove NAS100 toward its year high and US30 to a fresh intraday year high of 50,830 on Friday May 22. UK CPI April printed +2.8% YoY (below the 3.0% consensus and down from 3.3% in March, the softest reading since March 2025), advancing BoE pivot odds -- a GBP-negative print in isolation, but GBPCAD counter-intuitively recovered +1.1% from its 1.8302 low as the oil selloff weakened CAD more aggressively than soft CPI weakened GBP. Gold tested 4,465 intraday before recovering to 4,523 -- the correction from the 5,602 Iran-war ATH continues, with the 4,741 bullish recovery trigger now acting as a confirmed failed breakout.
