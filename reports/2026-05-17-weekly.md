## MACRO THEME

Two competing forces defined the week of May 11-17. The first was risk-on optimism driven by President Trump's high-stakes Beijing visit to meet President Xi Jinping (May 12-15), which lifted NAS100 to a year high of 29,678 on May 14 and pushed the Dow back above 50,000 (closing 50,063 on May 14). The week's epilogue arrived late Friday: Moody's stripped the United States of its last triple-A sovereign credit rating, downgrading from Aaa to Aa1 citing ballooning deficits and fiscal trajectory concerns. That downgrade will be the dominant pricing event at Monday's open (May 18) and is already shaping early currency and bond market moves seen in the live data. NVDA Q1 earnings Thursday May 22 remains the most pivotal equity event for the rest of the month.

**Tuesday May 12 -- US-China Summit Opens (Beijing):** Trump arrived in Beijing for meeting with Xi Jinping. Expectations ran high for tariff truce extension and Boeing aircraft purchases. Gold fell from 4,678 close Thursday to 4,555. Dow gave back 537 points. Inflation concerns dominated.

**Weekend May 16-17 -- Moody's Downgrade:** Moody's Ratings downgraded the United States sovereign credit from Aaa to Aa1, becoming the last of the three major rating agencies to strip the US of its top rating. The agency cited the ballooning federal deficit and long-term fiscal trajectory. The dollar fell on the news; gold, yen, and Swiss franc firmed in initial weekend/early Asian trading. This event is the dominant macro input for the week of May 18.
